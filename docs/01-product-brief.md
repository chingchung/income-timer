# 01 — Product brief

## One-line positioning

> Income Timer turns your salary into a live ticking number, so that every dollar
> you spend is felt the moment you spend it.

## Who it's for

**Primary — the salaried office worker who is bored.**
Hong Kong, 22-40, fixed monthly salary, fixed office hours, phone always within
reach. The core emotional moment is the dead hour at the desk: the number is
still going up even when the work feels pointless. This is the user who installs
it for the gimmick.

**Secondary — the person who overspends on small things.**
Same demographic, but the retention comes from the expense side. They already
failed at MoneyWiz / spreadsheet budgeting because logging felt like homework.
Here, logging is rewarded with an instant, visible consequence.

**Not for (v1)**: freelancers with irregular income, hourly shift workers with
rotating rosters, people wanting bank-account sync. All are real markets, all
add large complexity. See [roadmap](07-roadmap.md).

## The three gimmicks

1. **The rolling number.** An odometer that visibly climbs while you watch it.
   High earners see it spin fast; that is a feature, and it is shareable.
2. **Instant debit.** Spend $60 on lunch and the number drops $60 immediately,
   then recovers over the next ~14 minutes. You *watch* yourself pay it back.
3. **Price in time.** Every expense is shown in minutes of your working life.
   This is the line that ends up screenshotted and shared.

## Core loops

**Daily loop (the habit)**
Wake → glance at widget (0 or yesterday's close) → workday starts, number climbs
→ buy something → open app, 3-tap log → watch number dip and recover →
end of day, see net close.

**Weekly loop (the payoff)**
Sunday review: how many days closed positive, biggest single expense in minutes
of life, category breakdown, "you worked N hours purely to pay for food this week."

**The viral loop**
Share card: "我今日已經賺咗 $1,204.50" or "呢杯咖啡花咗我 9 分鐘人生".
Landing page celebrity comparison drives cold traffic.

## What v1 must do

- Set up income in under 60 seconds, no account, no bank linking.
- A home-screen widget that visibly changes through the day.
- Log an expense in 3 taps or fewer from the widget or lock screen.
- Show today's net, and never lie about the maths.

## What v1 must NOT do

- No sign-up, no email, no bank connection, no permissions beyond notifications.
- No budgets, no envelopes, no goal-setting. Those are the old, failed model.
- No income from investments, side hustles, multiple jobs. One salary, v1.

## Positioning against what exists

Salary-per-second calculators exist as one-off web toys; none of them persist,
and none of them tie to spending. Expense trackers exist in abundance; none of
them make you feel the money arriving. The combination — a live earnings clock
that your spending visibly dents — is the whole product.

The moat is not technical. It is the feeling, the animation quality, and the
copy. Budget design effort accordingly.

## Tone

Slightly dark, self-aware, Hong Kong office humour. The app knows that watching
your salary tick is a coping mechanism, and says so. Traditional Chinese first,
English second. Never motivational-guru. Never scolding about spending.
